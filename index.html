Key idea

Reactant particles must collide to react, but not every collision causes a reaction.

  • An effective collision is one that results in a reaction. The particles must collide with energy equal to or greater than the activation energy (Ea).
  • Ea is the minimum energy colliding particles need for bonds to break so the reaction can take place.
  • If the collision energy is less than Ea, the particles simply bounce apart unchanged.

At A-level, particles must also collide with the correct orientation. This simulation focuses on energy.

What to notice

  • Particles move at different speeds, so collisions happen with a range of energies.
  • Only collisions with energy ≥ Ea are effective (green). Most collisions are not (red).
  • Raise Ea: a smaller proportion of collisions is effective, so the reaction is slower.
Particles, speeds and energies are simplified for learning. Energy values are illustrative (kJ/mol).
